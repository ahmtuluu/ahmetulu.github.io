LED Resistor Calculator

Pick the LED colour, enter the supply voltage and current: you get the required resistor, a standard value you can buy and its power rating.

How is it calculated?

The voltage across an LED stays almost constant with current (the forward voltage, Vf). So a series resistor is needed to limit the current; the rest of the supply voltage drops across it.

R = (Vs − n · Vf) / IActual current: I = (Vs − n · Vf) / R(E24)Resistor power: P = (Vs − n · Vf) · In: number of LEDs in series

The calculated resistor is rarely a standard value. The tool suggests the next value up in the E24 series, so the LED current never exceeds your target. A power rating of at least twice the calculated power is recommended.

Typical Vf by colour

  • Red ≈ 2.0 V · orange / yellow ≈ 2.1 V · IR ≈ 1.3 V
  • Green, blue, white (InGaN) ≈ 3.0–3.2 V

These are typical values; the exact figure is in the LED datasheet and changes with current and temperature.

Quick table: LED resistor for 20 mA

One LED, 20 mA and the next standard value up in the E24 series. For an indicator LED, 5–10 mA is often bright enough and the LED lasts longer.

Frequently asked questions

What resistor do I need for an LED on 5V?

For 20 mA, a red LED (≈2.0 V) needs 150 Ω and a white/blue LED (≈3.1 V) needs 100 Ω. The common 220 Ω runs it a little below 20 mA, which is safe.

What resistor do I need for an LED on 12V?

For 20 mA: 510 Ω with a red LED, 470 Ω with a white LED. It dissipates about 0.2 W, so a 1/2 W resistor is safer.

Which leg does the LED resistor go on?

As long as it is in series, it can go on the anode (+, long leg) or the cathode (−) side; the current is the same.

Can several LEDs share one resistor?

LEDs in series can (if the supply is high enough). LEDs in parallel each need their own resistor, or the current will not share evenly.
