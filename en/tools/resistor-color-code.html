Resistor Color Code Calculator (4, 5, 6 Band)

Decode 4, 5 and 6-band resistor colour codes: pick the colours and see the value and tolerance. It also works backwards: type a value, get the colours.

How is it calculated?

Bands are read from left to right. The tolerance band is usually set slightly apart, on the right.

4 bands: [digit][digit] × [multiplier] [tolerance]5 bands: [digit][digit][digit] × [multiplier] [tolerance]6 bands: 5 bands + [temperature coefficient]Example: yellow-violet-red-gold = 47 × 100 = 4.7 kΩ ±5%

The “find colours from value” field rounds your value to 2 significant digits (4 bands) or 3 (5–6 bands) and picks the multiplier.

How to read a resistor colour code (4 steps)

  1. Find the direction: put the gold or silver band on the right. If the bands sit closer to one end, start reading from that end.
  2. Write down the digits: the first two bands (4-band) or three bands (5-band) are digits (black 0 … white 9).
  3. Apply the multiplier: the next band tells you how many zeros to add (red ×100, orange ×1 k, gold ×0.1).
  4. Read the tolerance: the last band is the precision: gold ±5%, silver ±10%, brown ±1%.

Example: brown-black-orange-gold → 1, 0, ×1000 → 10 kΩ ±5%. The same resistor with 5 bands is brown-black-black-red-brown.

Frequently asked questions

Which side do you read a resistor colour code from?

Read from left to right with the gold or silver tolerance band on the right. The tolerance band is often set slightly apart; if the bands sit closer to one end, start from that end. When in doubt, a multimeter is the surest check.

What is the colour code of a 10k resistor?

4-band 10 kΩ: brown-black-orange-gold. 5-band (±1%) 10 kΩ: brown-black-black-red-brown.

What are the colours of a 220 ohm resistor?

4 bands: red-red-brown-gold. 5 bands: red-red-black-black-brown.

What is the colour code of a 1k resistor?

4 bands: brown-black-red-gold. 5 bands: brown-black-black-brown-brown.

What do gold and silver bands mean?

As the last band, gold means ±5% and silver ±10% tolerance. As a multiplier, gold is ×0.1 and silver ×0.01, used on low values: 4.7 Ω = yellow-violet-gold-gold.

What is the difference between 4-band and 5-band resistors?

A 4-band resistor has 2 digits, a 5-band resistor has 3; 5-band parts are usually ±1% metal film resistors. A 6th band shows the temperature coefficient (ppm/K).
