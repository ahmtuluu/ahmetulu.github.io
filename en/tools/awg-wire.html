AWG ↔ mm² Wire Gauge Calculator

Convert an AWG number to a mm² cross-section, or find the AWG for the mm² you have. Diameter and copper resistance are calculated too.

How is it calculated?

AWG (American Wire Gauge) is a log scale: a higher number means a thinner wire, and every 6 gauges the diameter roughly halves.

d (mm) = 0.127 · 92^((36 − n) / 39)A = π · d² / 4R / m = ρ / A, ρ(Cu, 20 °C) = 1.7241·10⁻⁸ Ω·m00 → n = −1, 000 → n = −2, 0000 → n = −3

In “mm² → AWG” mode the thinnest AWG whose area is equal or larger than your value is suggested, so you never end up with a thinner wire.

Current capacity is not a single number: it depends on the insulation, whether wires are bundled and the ambient temperature.
