Basics

Ohm's Law Calculator

Enter any two of V, I, R and P and the other two are calculated instantly. Pick units (mA, kΩ, mW…) from the box next to each field.

How is it calculated?

Ohm's law links the voltage (V), current (I) and resistance (R) of a resistor. Combined with the power formula, knowing any two of the four quantities is enough to find the others.

V = I · RP = V · I = I² · R = V² / R

The tool solves these equations for the pair you pick. For example, with P and R known it first finds V = √(P·R), then I = √(P/R).

In practice

  • Pick a resistor rated for at least twice the calculated power (0.2 W dissipated → 1/2 W resistor).
  • Ohm's law applies to linear (ohmic) parts. It does not apply directly to LEDs or diodes; use the LED resistor tool for those.

Worked examples

  • Find the current: 5 V across 220 Ω gives I = 5 / 220 = 22.7 mA.
  • Find the resistance: a load drawing 2 A from 12 V is R = 12 / 2 = 6 Ω and uses P = 12 × 2 = 24 W.
  • Power limit: a 1/4 W, 1 kΩ resistor can carry at most I = √(P / R) = √(0.25 / 1000) ≈ 15.8 mA.

An easy way to remember it is the V-I-R triangle: cover the quantity you want; if the other two sit side by side multiply (V = I·R), if one is above the other divide (I = V/R, R = V/I).

Frequently asked questions

What is the Ohm's law formula?

V = I × R. Voltage (volts) = current (amps) × resistance (ohms). From it, I = V / R and R = V / I; for power use P = V × I.

What units does Ohm's law use?

Volts (V), amps (A), ohms (Ω) and watts (W). When using mA and kΩ, keep the units consistent: 5 V / 1 kΩ = 5 mA.

Does Ohm's law apply to an LED?

Not directly; an LED's voltage does not change linearly with current. Calculate a series resistor instead with the LED resistor tool.
